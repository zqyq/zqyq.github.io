I founded and have led the iMUSE Lab at Shenzhen University since 2022. The lab studies what a network of cameras can know about a large, moving scene: how many people are there, where each of them is, where they are going, and what happens next. The programme grew out of my doctoral work on wide-area multi-view counting and has since taken its own direction — calibration-free counting (ECCV’22) and optimal-transport localization (ECCV’24) gave way to a synthetic benchmark for the field (IJCV’26), diffusion-based crowd simulation (ICLR’26) and multi-view tracking (CVPR’26); the current frontier is 4D occupancy forecasting and generating the street scenes that train it.

Research

Full agenda →

Multi-view crowd intelligence

Counting, detection, localization, tracking and simulation of people seen by many cameras at once — fused onto one ground plane, without per-scene calibration, across views and scenes, and with as little labeled data as possible.

Core programme · {{ n_crowd }} papers

Dynamic 3D scene understanding

Choosing and trusting views: reconstruction-error-guided selection, completing a shape from a single image with a diffusion prior, recovering many people’s meshes under occlusion, and segmenting building instances at city scale.

Core programme · {{ n_3d }} papers

Emerging: street scenes and 4D occupancy

Camera-only forecasting of how occupied space evolves, and cross-view consistent generation of the street scenes that train and test such models.
