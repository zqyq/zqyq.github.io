I lead the iMUSE Lab at Shenzhen University. Our work asks how a fleet of cameras can understand crowds in large, real-world scenes — counting, localizing, tracking and simulating people — and how multi-view 3D reconstruction can be made robust to the viewpoints that produce it. This line runs from wide-area multi-view counting (CVPR’19, IJCV’22) through cross-scene and calibration-free counting (CVPR’21, ECCV’22), optimal-transport localization (ECCV’24) and a synthetic benchmark for the field (IJCV’26), to diffusion-based crowd simulation (ICLR’26) and multi-view crowd tracking (CVPR’26). The same machinery now reaches street scenes: cross-view consistent generation, camera-only 4D occupancy forecasting, and human mesh recovery at scene scale.

Research

Details →

Multi-view Crowd Analysis & Simulation

Counting, detection, localization, tracking and simulation of people in large scenes seen by many cameras — without per-scene calibration, and with benchmarks the community can build on.

{{ n_crowd }} listed paper{%- if n_crowd != 1 %}s{% endif %}

3D Reconstruction, Generation & Scene Understanding

Reconstruction-error-guided view selection for multi-view 3D objects, completing a shape from a single image with a diffusion prior, recovering many people’s meshes under occlusion, and segmenting building instances at city scale.

Autonomous Driving & Occupancy Forecasting

Camera-only driving perception: predicting how occupied space evolves by coupling 2D image evidence with 3D structure, and generating multi-view consistent street scenes.
