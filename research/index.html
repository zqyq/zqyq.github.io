Research

A camera network is the most scalable sensor for public space. We study what it can measure — how many, where, who is moving where, and what happens next — when the scene is large, the cameras are many, and the calibration you would like to have is exactly what you do not have.

Multi-view Crowd Analysis & Simulation

All papers →

Our through-line since the Ph.D.: fusing several views into one density map over a ground plane, so that a whole plaza, stadium or shoreline can be measured at once. Early work established wide-area counting with ground-plane density maps and multi-view fusion (CVPR 2019, extended in IJCV 2022), 3D-domain counting (AAAI 2020, IJCV 2022) and counting that transfers across views and scenes (CVPR 2021) or needs no camera calibration (ECCV 2022). We then moved from “how many” to “who and where”: people detection with learned view-wise weights (AAAI 2024), crowd localization by Mahalanobis-distance multi-view optimal transport (ECCV 2024), weakly-supervised calibration-free counting (PRCV 2025) and multi-view tracking under large real-world scenes (CVPR 2026). Two recent threads push the setting outward: a large synthetic benchmark that covers counting and localization together (IJCV 2026), and a diffusion model that simulates crowd motion with environmental conditioning and individual-group interaction (ICLR 2026). Label cost is the bottleneck in real deployments, so two further studies rank fusion models under partial labels and select the views worth labeling when the budget is small. None of it holds unless the cameras share a clock, which is why single-frame deep view synchronization and its dataset (TNNLS 2023) sit at the bottom of this stack.

Also in this group: RGB-T crowd counting with an external collaboration (ICASSP 2026).

3D Reconstruction, Generation & Scene Understanding

Multi-view reconstruction is only as good as the views you feed it. We select views by the reconstruction error they cause, which makes pipelines robust to view transformation (AAAI 2025), and we push the opposite direction too: recovering a full object from a single image by combining stable-diffusion generation with farthest-view selection (ICIG 2025), and recovering many people’s meshes in a large scene despite occlusion. Scene-level work targets the city itself — fine-grained building instance segmentation at scale (SIGGRAPH 2023, UrbanBIS) and its extension to adaptive region dividing with spatially-supervised contrastive learning.

Autonomous Driving & Occupancy Forecasting

Driving scenes are the hardest version of the same problem: many moving agents, no calibration you can trust, and predictions that must hold over time. We start from cameras only and couple 2D image evidence with 3D structure to forecast how occupied space evolves (InterOCF), and generate multi-view consistent street data to train and test such models.

Detection Beyond Crowds

The fusion machinery transfers to other counting and detection targets: Chinese white dolphins in the wild (ACM MMAsia 2021), which began as a conservation request, and tri-plane multi-view counting of silkie chickens (ChinaMM 2026, an undergraduate final-year project). The 2016 hyperspectral de-fencing paper is where the list begins.
